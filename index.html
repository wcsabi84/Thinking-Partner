Thinking Partner: Should I Move from Ireland to Bhutan?

A worked example of using AI not simply to answer a consequential life question, but to improve the question, pressure-test assumptions, calibrate confidence, research the evidence, and design a better decision process.

Note: This is a condensed and lightly edited reconstruction of a real conversation. The “hypotheses” step is made explicit here from ideas that emerged across several turns, so the reasoning method is visible rather than buried in the transcript.

Move 1 — Open with the real question

The consequential question
Me

Shall I move to Bhutan from Ireland?

ChatGPT

Don’t make the permanent move yet. Bhutan could be a remarkable fit for the right person, but the practical barriers—immigration, work, healthcare, distance and a much smaller labour market—make it a poor decision to treat as a simple lifestyle upgrade.

A better first step is to treat Bhutan as something to investigate seriously before giving up the option of remaining in Ireland.

Move 2 — Drill deeper: improve the frame

Drill-deeper turn 1
Me

What is a genuinely useful way to think about it?

ChatGPT

Don’t ask, “Is Bhutan better than Ireland?” Ask:

Which set of problems would I rather have?

Every place gives you something and charges you something. The important distinction is whether you are moving toward a life you understand or merely moving away from frustrations you hope geography will solve.

The useful tests are ordinary-life tests: What does Tuesday look like? What are your non-negotiables? Is the decision reversible? What would count as evidence that the move is succeeding or failing?

Drill-deeper turn 2
Me

What does the latest research actually say about this?

ChatGPT

The research does not strongly support the idea that moving to a “happier,” slower or more natural country will automatically make an individual happier.

What appears much more robust is that successful relocation depends heavily on belonging, social support, low loneliness, low acculturative stress, agency and person–environment fit. Nature can help wellbeing, but scenery is unlikely to compensate for social isolation, unsatisfying work or financial insecurity.

That changes the question from “Would I enjoy Bhutan?” to “Could I build a supported, sustainable ordinary life there?”

Move 3 — Ask the model what it would want to know

Ask-for-questions turn
Me

If you could ask any questions and explore every angle of this, what would you be most curious to ask?

ChatGPT

The most diagnostic questions are not “Which country is nicer?” but questions that could overturn the decision:

  • What are you hoping will feel different when you wake up in Bhutan?
  • What specifically about life in Ireland is wearing on you?
  • Why Bhutan, specifically?
  • Which sacrifices would you genuinely tolerate?
  • How important are career ambition, money, healthcare and proximity to family?
  • How easily do you build close relationships from scratch?
  • Would Bhutan still appeal if nobody thought it was special?
  • If you could get 80% of the life you imagine there without leaving Europe, would Bhutan itself still matter?

The central question became: Is Bhutan actually the destination, or is it carrying the meaning of a different life?

Move 4 — Make the competing hypotheses explicit

Hypotheses turn
Me

What hypotheses should I actually be testing before I decide?

ChatGPT
H1 — Bhutan-fit hypothesis: My dissatisfaction is substantially caused by a mismatch between my preferred way of living and my current environment. Bhutan offers conditions—pace, nature, scale, culture and community—that fit me materially better.
H2 — Projection hypothesis: Bhutan is functioning mainly as a symbol of simplicity, meaning, novelty or escape. The underlying problems would substantially follow me.
H3 — Belonging constraint: Even if Bhutan fits my values, difficulty building deep relationships or living as a long-term outsider could outweigh the benefits.
H4 — Opportunity-cost hypothesis: The career, healthcare, financial and mobility options I would give up in Ireland/EU matter more to my long-term wellbeing than I currently appreciate.
H5 — Option-value hypothesis: I do not need to choose permanently now. The best strategy may be to preserve my Irish/EU base while acquiring increasingly good evidence about Bhutan.
H6 — Third-option hypothesis: Another place or a redesigned life in Ireland could provide most of what I want at lower cost and risk.

Move 5 — Calibrate confidence instead of speaking in absolutes

Calibration turn
Me

State your confidence on each claim as high, medium, or low, and name the assumptions behind each one.

ChatGPT

Belonging and social support are likely to be central to relocation success.High
Assumption: migration and wellbeing findings generalise reasonably well to a voluntary Ireland→Bhutan move.

Ordinary-life experience is more informative than enjoying Bhutan as a visitor.High
Assumption: tourism overweights novelty, freedom from routine and scenery.

Your career trajectory could materially change whether Bhutan is a good choice.High
Assumption: work matters to your identity, finances or future options.

Small-society dynamics and outsider identity may matter greatly for you.Medium
Assumption: you care materially about privacy, cultural fluency and belonging; this is not yet known.

Bhutan is actually a better life choice for you than Ireland.Low
Assumption: too much personal evidence is still missing—especially work, finances, relationships, health, family plans and your lived experience of Bhutan.

The uncertainty was not a failure of the analysis. It was itself one of the most important findings.

Move 6 — Compress everything into one deep-research prompt

Deep-research prompt
Me

Give me a single deep-research prompt that encompasses everything we covered, then cross-validate it. Does this update your thinking? Anything unexpected?

ChatGPT 
Act as an independent, skeptical decision researcher. Evaluate whether moving
from Ireland to Bhutan would be a good life decision for me, with particular
emphasis on the next 5–10 years rather than only present conditions.

Do not assume that moving is desirable or undesirable. Do not romanticize
Bhutan or default to status-quo bias in favour of Ireland. Try actively to
falsify both “Bhutan would give me a better life” and “staying in Ireland is
safer/better.”

First diagnose the life problem, not just the geography:
- What feels missing in my current life?
- What do I expect Bhutan to provide psychologically, socially,
  professionally, spiritually, financially and physically?
- Why Bhutan specifically?
- Which problems are place-dependent and which are likely to follow me?
- What are my non-negotiables?
- What would my ideal ordinary week look like?

Then research the best available evidence on:
- relocation and subjective wellbeing;
- belonging, loneliness, social support and acculturation;
- person–environment fit, novelty, nature and meaning;
- Bhutan’s immigration, work, tax, healthcare and residency feasibility;
- Bhutan’s 5–10 year economic trajectory, youth employment, outward
  migration, hydropower, tourism and Gelephu Mindfulness City;
- Ireland’s 5–10 year trajectory in work, housing, healthcare,
  demographics and climate.

Distinguish facts, forecasts, policy targets, promotional claims and inference.

Construct realistic 2031 and 2036 scenarios for:
A. staying in Ireland;
B. living in established Bhutan such as Thimphu/Paro;
C. participating in the emerging Gelephu ecosystem;
D. the strongest third alternative.

Use decision theory: reversibility, option value, opportunity cost,
regret asymmetry and value of information.

For every major conclusion:
- state confidence as High, Medium or Low;
- state the assumptions behind it;
- identify evidence that would change the conclusion.

Finish with:
1. strongest case for moving;
2. strongest case against;
3. strongest third alternative;
4. current recommendation;
5. what would reverse the recommendation;
6. the three highest-value things to learn or test next.

The cross-check did not reverse the conclusion, but it sharpened it: do not make an irreversible move yet; build the capability to move if the evidence becomes compelling.

Three findings particularly mattered: Bhutan’s outward migration and youth-employment challenge weakened the romantic “better society” story; Bhutan’s emerging Gelephu project increased its long-term option value; and the migration literature made belonging look more consequential than scenery or national happiness branding.

Move 7 — Retrospect: what should I have asked from the start?

Retrospective
Me

What prompt should I have used from the very start, and what would have been the most useful questions to ask?

ChatGPT

The better opening would not have asked for a country verdict. It would have asked the model to diagnose the decision before solving it.

I’m considering whether to build a life in Bhutan rather than Ireland. Don’t begin by comparing the countries or giving me a recommendation. First, treat this as a life-design and decision-under-uncertainty problem. Interview me to determine what I am actually trying to change, what I value, what I may be projecting onto Bhutan, and what constraints or trade-offs I may be underestimating.

The five highest-value questions were:

  1. What are you actually trying to change?
  2. Why does that require Bhutan?
  3. What would you lose that you currently underestimate?
  4. Can you realistically belong there?
  5. What is the cheapest experiment that could prove you wrong?

Three-line reflection

The move that surprised me most was calibration: asking for confidence levels exposed how much of an apparently confident recommendation was actually conditional on missing personal information.

It shifted the goal from “get the right answer” to identify which uncertainties matter enough to investigate.

The most valuable outcome was not a verdict about Bhutan; it was a better method for making consequential decisions without confusing confidence, evidence and personal fit.

What I learned

The original question looked geographical: Ireland or Bhutan? The deeper question was about life design: What kind of life am I trying to build, which environment supports it, and what evidence would justify paying the costs of changing?

The strongest current strategy is therefore not an irreversible binary choice. It is to preserve valuable options, seek disconfirming evidence, and design a real-world test that reveals whether the imagined life survives contact with ordinary life.
